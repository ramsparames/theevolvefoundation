Choices · Making decisions

You don't need a career plan to make a good next choice

A young person does not need to know exactly where they are going to make a thoughtful decision about what to explore next.

By Rams Parameswaran, Founder2 October 2026 · 2 min read
THE IDEA

We often make the next decision feel bigger than it needs to be.

Which course should I choose? Which internship should I apply for? What career should I build?

For a young person, these questions can sound as though one decision is going to determine everything that comes after it.

You can make a good decision without knowing the whole destination.

At 19 or 20, you are making choices with limited information. You have had only a handful of experiences. You are still discovering what you enjoy, what you are good at, what kind of environment brings out your best and what you do not want.

That does not mean you should avoid making decisions. It means the decision should match what you actually know today.

Think about the next direction, not the final destination.

A useful question is not, “What do I want to do for the rest of my life?”

It is, “What is worth exploring next?”

That shift creates room to learn. You can choose a project because the problem interests you. Take an internship because it gives you exposure to a kind of work you want to understand. Speak to someone in a field you are curious about. Try something and pay attention to what you learn from it.

Each experience gives you more information about yourself and about the world of work.

The next choice can be a test.

You do not have to be certain before you move. You need enough clarity to take a thoughtful next step and enough openness to learn from what happens.

That is very different from having a perfect career plan.

Sometimes a good decision is simply one that gives you better information for the decision that comes after it.
