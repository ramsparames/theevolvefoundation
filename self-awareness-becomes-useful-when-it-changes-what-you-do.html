Self-awareness becomes useful when it changes what you do

Knowing your strengths is only the starting point. The next step is turning that understanding into possibilities worth testing.

By Rams Parameswaran, Founder 22 September 2026 · 3 min read

Knowing your strengths is useful, but it does not automatically tell you what to do next.

You may know that you are good at solving problems, explaining things clearly, bringing people together or creating structure. That gives you a better understanding of yourself, but each of those strengths could be applied in many different directions.

Knowing yourself does not automatically give you direction.

This is where self-awareness has to move beyond simply identifying strengths. A useful next question is: What draws my attention? What do I find myself reading about, thinking about or wanting to understand better? What kinds of problems make me curious? What work or conversations seem to hold my interest?

Interests are not answers, and they do not have to become careers. But they are useful signals. When you begin to put together what you are good at with what you are naturally curious about, you start to see possibilities that may not have been obvious before.

The temptation then is to ask which possibility is the right one. I think that question comes too early.

A better question

Which possibility is worth testing?

Testing turns possibilities into information.

Testing does not have to mean making a big commitment. It could mean speaking to someone who works in that area, trying a small project, taking a course, volunteering or building something. The purpose is simply to get closer to the possibility and learn from it.

Does your interest grow once you experience it more closely? Does the work make use of your strengths? Does it lead you towards something else you had not considered?

You do not need to know exactly where a strength will lead before you use it. You need enough understanding of yourself to choose the next direction worth exploring.

That is when self-awareness becomes useful — when it starts shaping what you choose to do next.
