COMPASS · BEFORE COLLEGE

Make a better-informed choice.

A guided decision-making experience for students approaching college and thinking through what to study and where to study.

Compass will not tell you which course or college to choose. It helps you understand what matters to you, look carefully at the options in front of you and make a decision you can explain.

Guided digital experience·For students approaching college·Student + parent output
THE THINKING BEHIND COMPASS

Choosing well is not the same as choosing perfectly.

At 17 or 18, students are often expected to make a big decision before they have had much opportunity to think about what they actually want from the next few years. Compass is designed to slow that decision down just enough to make it more thoughtful.

It won't tell you what to choose. It will help you make a better-informed choice.
HOW COMPASS WILL WORK

Start with yourself. End with a decision you can explain.

Compass moves from reflection to real choices. The questions are designed to help students think, not to label them or assign them a career.

01

Know Me

Notice what you enjoy, what you seem to bring and what matters to you.

02

What Am I Looking For?

Define what you want from your course and your college experience.

03

Explore My Options

Bring the real courses and colleges you are considering into the conversation.

04

Check the Fit

Look at what fits, what does not and what you still need to find out.

05

My Compass

Pull your thinking together, identify your next steps and make the decision clearer.

WHAT YOU WILL LEAVE WITH

Not a score. A clearer way to think.

The final Compass output is designed to be useful to the student and to start a better conversation at home.

My Compass

What matters to me, what I am considering, what I still need to find out, what I am leaning towards and what I will do next.

Your Child's Compass

A parent-friendly summary of what the student is considering and where they still have questions, without turning the student's thinking into a recommendation.

A conversation worth having

Questions that help students and parents discuss the decision without one person simply making it for the other.
