Recalculating

“I changed my mind. Did I waste my time?”

— THE STORY 60–75 seconds

You're driving somewhere and you miss the turn.

Your GPS doesn't say: “That was a terrible decision. The journey is ruined.”

It simply says: “Recalculating.”

It looks at where you are now and works out another route.

We're often much harsher with ourselves.

We choose something. We learn more. Maybe it isn't what we expected.

And immediately we think: “I made the wrong choice.”

Perhaps.

But perhaps you also have information now that you didn't have when you started.

Changing direction after learning something is not always evidence that the earlier decision was a mistake.

Sometimes it is evidence that you are paying attention.

So if something you chose no longer feels right, the question may not be:

“Why did I get this wrong?”

It may simply be:

“Given what I know now, where do I go from here?”

The idea

Changing direction after learning something can be evidence of attention and adaptation, not evidence that the earlier choice was a failure.

A question to take with you

Given what I know now, where do I go from here?

Keep exploring

New information can justify a new route.
