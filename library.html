THE EVOLVE FOUNDATION LIBRARY

Ideas for when you're figuring things out.

Short ideas, stories and perspectives to help you see a situation differently and decide what you want to do next.

10 IDEASSimple enough to remember. Useful enough to create movement.

BROWSE

Start with the one that catches your attention.

There is no required order. Pick the question that sounds familiar.

Driving Through Fog

“I don’t know where this will lead.”

Uncertainty does not necessarily mean you are lost. Sometimes clarity appears because you move.

Read the idea →

Confidence Is a Receipt

“I don’t feel confident enough to begin.”

Confidence may be something we build from evidence after acting, rather than something we need to possess before acting.

Read the idea →

Nobody Else Asked

“Everyone else seems to understand except me.”

Silence is not proof that everybody else understands.

Read the idea →

One Data Point Is Not the Graph

“I failed. Maybe I’m just not good at this.”

A single result can contain useful information without being enough evidence for a conclusion about your identity or ability.

Read the idea →

The Dressing Room

“What if I try something and discover it isn’t right for me?”

Exploring a possibility is not the same as committing to it. Testing can create information.

Read the idea →

Recalculating

“I changed my mind. Did I waste my time?”

Changing direction after learning something can be evidence of attention and adaptation, not evidence that the earlier choice was a failure.

Read the idea →

The Other Lane

“Everyone seems to be doing better than me.”

Comparison can provide information, but becomes damaging when another person’s journey becomes the scoreboard for your own.

Read the idea →

The First Domino

“I know what I should do. I just can’t get started.”

Large goals often become actionable only when translated into the smallest move that creates momentum.

Read the idea →

A Clue, Not a Contract

“I’m interested in something, but does that mean it should become my career?”

Interest is useful as a signal for exploration without needing to make a promise about the future.

Read the idea →

Who’s Holding the Wheel?

“I’m scared, so maybe I shouldn’t do it.”

Fear and doubt can be noticed and listened to without automatically becoming the decision-maker.
